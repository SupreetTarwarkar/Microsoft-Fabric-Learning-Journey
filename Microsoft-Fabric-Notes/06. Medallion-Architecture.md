# Chapter 6: Medallion Architecture

---

## 6.1 What is Medallion Architecture?

**Medallion Architecture is a layered data design pattern used to progressively improve data quality as data moves through different stages.**

The three main layers are:

> **Bronze → Silver → Gold**

A simple understanding is:

> **Bronze = Raw Data**  
> **Silver = Cleaned / Validated Data**  
> **Gold = Curated / Business-Ready Data**

### Simple Understanding

**Bronze**

Stores what came from the source.

↓

**Silver**

Cleans, validates and standardizes the data.

↓

**Gold**

Prepares the data for business analysis and reporting.

### Complete Flow

```text
Source Data
     ↓
Bronze
Raw / Source-Aligned
     ↓
Silver
Cleaned / Validated
     ↓
Gold
Curated / Business-Ready
     ↓
Semantic Model / Power BI
```

The important idea is that data becomes progressively more useful as it moves through the layers.

---

## 6.2 Why Use Medallion Architecture?

The main purpose of Medallion Architecture is to avoid mixing:

- Raw source data
- Cleaned data
- Business logic
- Aggregations
- Reporting structures

inside the same layer.

Without separation, a table could contain:

- Raw source records
- Partially cleaned records
- Business calculations
- Aggregations
- Reporting logic

This makes the solution more difficult to:

- Understand
- Maintain
- Troubleshoot
- Audit
- Reprocess

Medallion Architecture separates these responsibilities.

### Simple Flow

```text
Source Data
     ↓
Bronze
Raw Data
     ↓
Silver
Cleaned and Validated Data
     ↓
Gold
Curated and Business-Ready Data
     ↓
Power BI / Analytics
```

### Benefits of Medallion Architecture

#### Better Data Quality

Data improves progressively as it moves through the layers.

#### Easier Troubleshooting

We can identify whether an issue started in:

- Source
- Bronze
- Silver
- Gold

#### Reusability

Clean Silver data can support multiple Gold datasets.

#### Auditability

Raw source-aligned data can be preserved in Bronze.

#### Separation of Responsibilities

Ingestion, cleaning and business logic remain separated.

#### Scalability

The architecture can support increasingly complex data solutions.

#### Better Analytics

Gold provides curated data designed for business consumption.

### Important Architecture Mindset

> **Architecture should solve the business and data problem, not simply follow a diagram.**

Bronze-Silver-Gold is a useful pattern, but it should not be treated as an absolute requirement for every small project.

---

## 6.3 Bronze Layer

The **Bronze Layer contains raw or minimally processed data received from source systems**.

Typical Bronze characteristics include:

- Source-aligned data
- Minimal transformations
- Original values retained
- Useful for reprocessing
- Useful for auditing
- Useful for troubleshooting

### Example

Source data:

| OrderID | Status | Revenue | OrderDate |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 101 | Completed | 500 | 01-08-2026 |
| 102 | Cancelled | 600 | 01-08-2026 |
| 103 | Returned | 300 | 01-08-2026 |
| 104 | Completed | 0 | 01-08-2026 |

Bronze may preserve all these records.

### Important Principle

> **Bronze should preserve source-aligned data rather than immediately applying heavy business logic.**

### Source to Bronze Responsibility

The main responsibility between Source and Bronze is generally **ingestion**.

Examples:

- Copy source files
- Preserve original data
- Capture source metadata
- Retain source-aligned values

### Important Rule — Do Not Apply Every Transformation in Bronze

If we immediately:

- Remove records
- Rename everything
- Aggregate data
- Apply business rules
- Delete source values

inside Bronze, we reduce the value of having a raw layer.

A better separation is:

> **Bronze → Preserve**  
> **Silver → Clean**  
> **Gold → Business**

---

## 6.4 Silver Layer

The **Silver Layer contains cleaned, validated and standardized data**.

In my practical workflow, Bronze-to-Silver transformation included:

- Removing cancelled transactions
- Removing returned transactions
- Removing zero-revenue transactions
- Correcting data types such as Order Date
- Validating the cleaned result

### Example

Bronze:

| OrderID | Status | Revenue |
|---:|---|---:|
| 101 | Completed | 500 |
| 102 | Cancelled | 600 |
| 103 | Returned | 300 |
| 104 | Completed | 0 |
| 105 | Completed | 900 |

After applying the business cleaning rules:

Silver:

| OrderID | Status | Revenue |
|---:|---|---:|
| 101 | Completed | 500 |
| 105 | Completed | 900 |

### Simple Understanding

> **Silver = Trusted and cleaned version of Bronze data**

### Bronze to Silver Responsibility

Bronze-to-Silver transformation mainly focuses on **data quality**.

Examples:

- Remove invalid records
- Fix data types
- Remove duplicates
- Standardize formats
- Handle null values
- Apply validation rules

### Silver is a Reusable Foundation

Silver should not be designed only for one particular report.

A well-designed Silver layer can support multiple downstream business use cases.

---

## 6.5 Gold Layer

The **Gold Layer contains curated data prepared for business consumption and analytics**.

Gold is the layer closest to:

- Power BI
- Business reporting
- KPI calculations
- Analytical models
- Semantic models
- Business users

Gold should be designed according to **business requirements**, rather than simply copying Silver data.

### Most Important Rule of Gold

> **Gold is created according to how the business needs to analyze the data.**

Suppose Silver contains detailed transaction-level data.

Gold may create business-oriented datasets such as:

- Sales by Product
- Revenue by Region
- Monthly Sales Summary
- Customer-level metrics
- Product performance
- Business KPI tables

### Example

Silver:

| OrderID | Product | Region | Quantity | Revenue |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| 101 | Laptop | West | 1 | 70000 |
| 102 | Mobile | East | 2 | 60000 |
| 103 | Laptop | West | 1 | 70000 |

Gold:

| Product | Total Revenue | Total Orders |
|---|---:|---:|
| Laptop | 140000 | 2 |
| Mobile | 60000 | 1 |

The Gold dataset is now easier to use for business reporting.

### Gold is Not Just "More Clean Data"

Silver mainly focuses on:

- Data quality
- Validation
- Standardization
- Deduplication
- Correct data types

Gold mainly focuses on:

- Business logic
- Aggregations
- Business entities
- Reporting structures
- Analytical performance
- KPIs

Therefore:

> **Silver makes data trustworthy. Gold makes data useful for the business.**

### Gold Should Be Based on Business Requirements

Before designing Gold, ask:

- What KPIs are required?
- What dimensions are needed?
- What level of detail is required?
- Which reports will consume the data?
- What calculations are repeatedly needed?
- What business questions need to be answered?

Example requirement:

Management wants:

- Revenue by Month
- Revenue by Region
- Total Orders
- Average Order Value
- Product Performance

Gold should therefore be designed to support those analytical requirements.

### Example Business KPIs from Gold

**Total Revenue**

```text
SUM(Revenue)
```

**Total Orders**

```text
COUNT(OrderID)
```

**Average Order Value**

```text
Total Revenue / Total Orders
```

**Quantity Sold**

```text
SUM(Quantity)
```

**Revenue by Product**

```text
SUM(Revenue) grouped by Product
```

**Revenue by Region**

```text
SUM(Revenue) grouped by Region
```

### Important Rule — Gold Should Not Become Another Raw Layer

Gold should have a clear analytical or consumer purpose.

Creating a Gold table that is simply an exact duplicate of Silver without a business reason may add little value.

Before creating Gold, ask:

> **What business problem is this Gold dataset solving?**

## 6.6 Bronze vs Silver vs Gold

The easiest way to understand Medallion Architecture is to compare the responsibilities of the three layers.

| Layer | Main Purpose | Typical Data |
|---|---|---|
| **Bronze** | Preserve raw source data | Raw / source-aligned records |
| **Silver** | Clean and validate data | Standardized, validated and deduplicated data |
| **Gold** | Serve business analytics | Curated and business-ready data |

### Easy Way to Remember

> **Bronze = Raw**

> **Silver = Clean**

> **Gold = Business**

### Transformation Responsibility

```text
Source → Bronze
       Ingestion / Preservation

Bronze → Silver
       Data Quality / Validation

Silver → Gold
       Business Logic / Analytics
```

### Detailed Comparison

**Bronze**

Focuses on:

- Preserving source data
- Raw ingestion
- Reprocessing
- Auditability

**Silver**

Focuses on:

- Cleaning
- Validation
- Standardization
- Deduplication
- Correct data types

**Gold**

Focuses on:

- Business logic
- KPIs
- Aggregation
- Fact and dimension structures
- Analytical consumption

## 6.7 Data Quality Across Layers

Medallion Architecture represents progressively improving data quality.

Conceptually:

```text
Bronze
Lowest Refinement
     ↓
Silver
Higher Quality
     ↓
Gold
Highest Business Readiness
```

This does **not** mean Bronze data is bad.

Bronze is intentionally kept close to the source.

Each layer has a different purpose.

### Important Distinction

The goal is not:

```text
Bad Data → Better Data → Perfect Data
```

A better understanding is:

```text
Raw Data
   ↓
Trusted Data
   ↓
Business-Ready Data
```

### Real-World Understanding

The important learning is not simply:

> **I know Bronze, Silver and Gold.**

A stronger understanding is:

> **I use Medallion Architecture to separate raw ingestion, data-quality transformations and business-ready analytics. Bronze preserves source-aligned data, Silver provides cleaned and validated reusable data, and Gold creates curated datasets based on business requirements.**

---

## 6.8 Why Keep Bronze?

A common question is:

> **If Silver is already cleaned, why keep Bronze?**

Bronze may still be required for:

- Reprocessing
- Debugging
- Auditing
- New transformation rules
- Recovering from transformation errors
- Comparing source data with transformed data

### Example

Suppose a Silver transformation incorrectly removes valid orders.

If Bronze still exists:

```text
Bronze
   ↓
Fix Notebook Logic
   ↓
Reprocess Data
   ↓
Recreate Silver
```

Without Bronze, the original source-aligned data may no longer be easily available.

### Main Reason

> **Bronze preserves the ability to go back to what originally arrived from the source.**

---

## 6.9 Why Keep Silver?

Another common question is:

> **If Gold is the final business layer, why keep Silver?**

Because Silver provides a **reusable clean foundation**.

Multiple Gold datasets can be created from the same Silver dataset.

For example:

```text
Silver Sales
     ↓
Gold Product Performance
     ↓
Gold Regional Sales
     ↓
Gold Customer Analytics
     ↓
Gold Monthly Revenue
```

We do not need to rebuild the same cleaning logic separately for every business report.

### Simple Understanding

> **Silver = Reusable Trusted Data**

> **Gold = Consumer / Business-Specific Data**

Silver therefore becomes an important reusable layer between raw ingestion and business-specific analytics.

---

## 6.10 Multiple Gold Tables

One Silver dataset can support multiple Gold datasets.

### Example

Silver:

```text
silver_sales
```

contains clean transaction-level sales data.

From it we may create:

```text
gold_product_sales
```

```text
gold_customer_sales
```

```text
gold_region_sales
```

```text
gold_monthly_sales
```

Conceptually:

```text
                    → Gold Product Sales
                   /
Silver Sales  ────→ Gold Customer Sales
                   \
                    → Gold Regional Sales
                     \
                      → Gold Monthly Sales
```

### Why This is Useful

This improves:

- Reusability
- Consistency
- Maintainability
- Separation of business use cases

The data-cleaning logic is performed in Silver once and reused across multiple analytical outputs.

### Multiple Lakehouse Implementations

There is more than one way to physically organize Medallion layers.

#### Option 1 — One Lakehouse

One Lakehouse may contain organized Bronze, Silver and Gold areas or tables.

#### Option 2 — Separate Lakehouses

For example:

```text
lh_bronze
    ↓
lh_silver
    ↓
lh_gold
```

There is no absolute rule that every Fabric project must create exactly three separate Lakehouses.

The physical architecture can depend on:

- Project complexity
- Security
- Team structure
- Data volume
- Governance
- Organizational standards

## 6.11 Gold Aggregations

Gold frequently contains aggregated data.

Suppose Silver contains:

```text
1,000,000 Transaction Rows
```

Gold might contain:

| Month | Region | Total Revenue | Total Orders |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Aug-2026 | West | 5000000 | 12000 |
| Aug-2026 | East | 4200000 | 10500 |

This can reduce the amount of detailed processing required for certain business reports.

Instead of repeatedly scanning large transaction-level datasets, a curated Gold table can provide the analytical structure required by the report.

### Important — Gold Does Not Always Mean Aggregation

Gold **often** contains aggregated data, but Gold does not have to be aggregated in every situation.

The main requirement is:

> **Gold should be curated and optimized for its intended analytical use case.**

For example, a Gold fact table may still contain transaction-level rows if the semantic model needs that level of detail.

Therefore:

> **Gold = Business-Ready**

not necessarily:

> **Gold = Always Aggregated**

## 6.12 Granularity in Gold

**Granularity means the level of detail stored in a table.**

Examples include:

### Transaction Level

One row per transaction or order.

### Daily Level

One row per day.

### Monthly Level

One row per month.

### Customer Level

One row per customer.

When designing Gold, we need to ask:

> **At what level does the business need to analyze the data?**

### Example

Suppose management only requires:

> **Monthly Revenue by Region**

A Gold table might contain:

| Month | Region | Revenue |
|---|---|---:|
| Aug-2026 | West | 5000000 |
| Aug-2026 | East | 4200000 |

For this particular use case, every individual transaction row may not be necessary in the Gold dataset.

### Important

Granularity should be chosen according to:

- Reporting requirements
- KPI requirements
- Semantic model requirements
- Required level of analysis

## 6.14 Delta Tables Across Layers

Delta tables can be used throughout Medallion Architecture.

Conceptually:

```text
Bronze Delta
     ↓
Silver Delta
     ↓
Gold Delta
```

This provides useful Delta capabilities across the data layers, including:

- ACID transactions
- Schema management
- Versioning
- Efficient Parquet-based storage
- Reliable updates and deletes

### Fabric Flow

```text
Source
  ↓
Pipeline
  ↓
Bronze Delta
  ↓
Notebook / PySpark
  ↓
Silver Delta
  ↓
Notebook / SQL / Dataflow Gen2
  ↓
Gold Delta
  ↓
Semantic Model / Power BI
```

### Pipeline and Notebook Relationship

A Fabric Medallion workflow can combine multiple tools.

**Pipeline**

Handles:

- Orchestration
- Data movement
- Scheduling

**Notebook**

Handles:

- PySpark transformations
- Data-quality logic
- Complex processing

**SQL / Dataflow Gen2**

Can also be used for business transformations depending on the requirement.

Therefore:

> **Pipeline orchestrates the movement; transformation tools improve data through the layers.**

### Naming Conventions

Clear naming can help distinguish the layers.

Possible examples:

**Bronze**

```text
brz_sales
```

or:

```text
bronze_sales
```

**Silver**

```text
slv_sales
```

or:

```text
silver_sales
```

**Gold**

```text
gold_product_sales
gold_customer_sales
```

There is no single mandatory naming convention.

The important rule is:

> **Use a consistent naming standard across the project.**

## 6.15 Data Validation Between Layers

Data should be validated when it moves between Medallion layers.

A transformation is not complete simply because the process executed successfully.

### Example

Bronze:

```text
100,000 Records
```

Silver:

```text
90,000 Records
```

Then we should understand why **10,000 records were removed**.

Suppose the business rules explain:

```text
4,000 Cancelled
+
3,000 Returned
+
3,000 Zero Revenue
=
10,000 Removed
```

The row-count difference can now be explained.

### Validation Questions

When moving Bronze → Silver, ask:

- How many records entered?
- How many records were removed?
- Why were they removed?
- Were duplicates handled correctly?
- Were null values handled correctly?
- Were data types corrected?
- Were the expected business rules applied?

### Practical Principle

> **Successful execution does not automatically mean correct data.**

### Practical Example

Suppose an online retailer provides daily transactions.

Bronze:

| OrderID | Product | Status | Revenue |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 101 | Laptop | Completed | 70000 |
| 102 | Mobile | Cancelled | 30000 |
| 103 | Tablet | Returned | 20000 |
| 104 | Mobile | Completed | 40000 |

After applying cleaning rules:

Silver:

| OrderID | Product | Status | Revenue |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 101 | Laptop | Completed | 70000 |
| 104 | Mobile | Completed | 40000 |

Then Gold may aggregate the cleaned data:

| Product | Total Revenue | Total Orders |
|---|---:|---:|
| Laptop | 70000 | 1 |
| Mobile | 40000 | 1 |

Finally, Power BI may visualize:

- Total Revenue
- Revenue by Product
- Number of Orders
- Sales Trend

This demonstrates the practical purpose of the Medallion Architecture.

## 6.16 Gold Validation

Gold data also needs to be validated.

Suppose Silver contains total revenue of:

```text
₹50 Million
```

After aggregating the data into Gold, the total revenue represented by Gold should reconcile to the expected value unless specific filters or business rules were intentionally applied.

Therefore:

> **Aggregated data should reconcile with its underlying detailed data.**

### Gold Validation Checks

Possible validation checks include:

- Total Revenue reconciliation
- Total Orders reconciliation
- Quantity reconciliation
- Number of customers
- Number of products
- Business-rule validation
- Expected aggregation grain
- Correct dimensional mapping

### Example

Suppose Silver contains:

```text
Total Revenue = ₹50 Million
```

Gold contains:

```text
West = ₹20 Million
East = ₹15 Million
North = ₹10 Million
South = ₹5 Million
```

Then:

```text
₹20M + ₹15M + ₹10M + ₹5M = ₹50M
```

The Gold aggregation reconciles with Silver.

### Important Principle

> **Business-ready data still needs validation.**

Gold should not be trusted simply because it is the final analytical layer.

## 6.17 Interview Understanding

### What is Medallion Architecture?

Medallion Architecture is a layered data design pattern in which data progressively improves through:

> **Bronze → Silver → Gold**

Bronze stores raw or source-aligned data, Silver contains cleaned and validated data, and Gold contains curated business-ready datasets.

### What is the purpose of Bronze?

Bronze preserves raw or source-aligned data so that it can be:

- Reprocessed
- Audited
- Troubleshot
- Compared with transformed data

### What is the purpose of Silver?

Silver provides:

- Cleaned
- Validated
- Standardized
- Reusable

data that can support multiple analytical use cases.

### What is the purpose of Gold?

Gold contains curated data designed according to business requirements and optimized for:

- Analytics
- Semantic models
- Reporting
- KPIs
- Business consumption

### Why not directly load Bronze data into Power BI?

Bronze may still contain:

- Invalid transactions
- Incorrect data types
- Duplicates
- Null values
- Inconsistent formats
- Raw source values

Using Silver and Gold creates a more reliable analytical solution.

### Why do we need both Silver and Gold?

Silver provides a reusable trusted foundation.

Gold applies business-specific logic and analytical structure.

Therefore:

> **Silver = Reusable Trusted Data**

> **Gold = Consumer / Business-Ready Data**

### Does Gold always contain aggregated data?

No.

Gold can contain aggregations, but it may also contain detailed fact-level data when that level of detail is required for analytics.

The main requirement is:

> **Gold should be curated for its intended business use case.**

### Do Bronze, Silver and Gold always require three separate Lakehouses?

No.

The physical implementation can vary depending on:

- Security
- Governance
- Project complexity
- Team structure
- Data volume
- Organizational standards

### What is the difference between Bronze-to-Silver and Silver-to-Gold transformation?

**Bronze → Silver**

Primarily focuses on:

- Data quality
- Validation
- Cleaning
- Standardization

**Silver → Gold**

Primarily focuses on:

- Business logic
- Aggregations
- KPIs
- Fact/dimension creation
- Analytical structures

### Why is validation important between layers?

Because a technically successful transformation can still produce incorrect business results.

Each layer should be validated against:

- Expected row counts
- Business rules
- Important metrics
- Reconciliation checks

## 6.18 Key Takeaways

1. **Medallion Architecture organizes data into Bronze, Silver and Gold layers.**

2. The easiest rule to remember is:

   > **Bronze = Raw**  
   > **Silver = Clean**  
   > **Gold = Business**

3. Bronze preserves source-aligned data for:
   - Reprocessing
   - Auditing
   - Troubleshooting

4. Silver contains:
   - Cleaned data
   - Validated data
   - Standardized data
   - Deduplicated data

5. My practical Bronze-to-Silver transformation included:
   - Removing cancelled transactions
   - Removing returned transactions
   - Removing zero-revenue transactions
   - Correcting data types

6. Gold contains curated, business-ready data for analytics and reporting.

7. The most important Gold design rule is:

   > **Build Gold according to business requirements.**

8. Silver mainly focuses on **data quality**, while Gold mainly focuses on **business logic and analytical consumption**.

9. One Silver dataset can support **multiple Gold datasets**.

10. Gold can contain:
    - Aggregations
    - KPIs
    - Fact tables
    - Dimension tables
    - Business-specific datasets

11. **Gold does not always have to be aggregated.**

12. Granularity determines the level of detail stored in a Gold table.

13. Gold can support analytical structures such as **Star Schema**.

14. Delta tables can be used throughout the Medallion Architecture:

```text
Bronze Delta
     ↓
Silver Delta
     ↓
Gold Delta
```

15. Bronze, Silver and Gold do **not necessarily require three separate Lakehouses**.

16. Transformation responsibilities can be remembered as:

   > **Source → Bronze = Ingestion / Preservation**  
   > **Bronze → Silver = Data Quality**  
   > **Silver → Gold = Business Logic**

17. Data should be **validated between layers**, not simply transformed.

18. Gold aggregations should reconcile with the underlying Silver data unless intentional business filters explain the difference.

19. A useful Fabric implementation pattern is:

```text
Source
   ↓
Pipeline
   ↓
Bronze
   ↓
Notebook / PySpark
   ↓
Silver
   ↓
Notebook / SQL / Dataflow Gen2
   ↓
Gold
   ↓
Semantic Model / Power BI
```

20. The complete architectural flow to remember is:

> **Source → Bronze → Clean & Validate → Silver → Business Logic → Gold → Semantic Model / Power BI**

21. The most important architectural mindset is:

> **Preserve raw data, create reusable trusted data, then build business-ready data from it.**
